Bandhavgarh
National Park

Bandhavgarh National Park is one of the most renowned national parks of Central India. It is located in the Umaria district of Madhya Pradesh, among the Vindhya hills, and was declared a national park in 1968. Earlier, it was a hunting ground of the Maharajas of Rewa.


Bandhavgarh Tiger Reserve is famous for its tigers, and its core area (Bandhavgarh National Park and Panpatha Sanctuary) spreads over about 716 sq km. The park is divided into three main safari zones: Tala, Magdhi and Khitauli, where tigers can often be spotted during morning and evening safaris.


Bandhavgarh is also known for the ancient Bandhavgarh Fort, which stands on a hill inside the park, along with old caves and statues. Besides tigers, the park is home to leopards, sloth bears, deer, gaur and many species of birds, so a visit here is a treat for every wildlife and nature lover.


The park is best visited between October and March, and it remains closed for tourists during the monsoon season. The nearest major airport is Jabalpur, about 200 km away.
